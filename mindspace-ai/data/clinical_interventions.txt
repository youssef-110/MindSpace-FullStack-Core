=== CLINICAL PSYCHOLOGY & THERAPEUTIC INTERVENTIONS CORPUS ===

COGNITIVE BEHAVIORAL THERAPY (CBT) FRAMEWORK
1. The Cognitive Model:
Situation -> Automatic Thoughts -> Core Beliefs / Schemas -> Somatic & Emotional Reaction -> Behavioral Action.

2. Common Cognitive Distortions:
- All-or-Nothing Thinking: Evaluating situations in black-and-white dichotomies ("If I'm not perfect, I am a total failure").
- Catastrophizing: Presuming the worst-case scenario will inevitably unfold without evidence.
- Mind Reading & Fortune Telling: Believing one knows others' thoughts without verification, or predicting negative outcomes.
- Emotional Reasoning: Assuming negative subjective feelings reflect objective external truth ("I feel overwhelmed, so the situation must be impossible").
- Mental Filtering & Disqualifying the Positive: Fixating on a single flaw while dismissing all accomplishments.
- Overgeneralization: Drawing sweeping global conclusions from a single isolated incident.
- "Should" Statements: Moralistic self-demands that breed guilt, resentment, and chronic inadequacy.

3. Core CBT Interventions:
- Thought Record: Identifying situation, automatic thought, emotional intensity (0-100%), evidence for, evidence against, and balanced adaptive reframe.
- Behavioral Activation: Systematically scheduling mastery and pleasure activities to counteract depressive inertia and restore dopaminergic reward sensitivity.
- Downward Arrow Technique: Uncovering core beliefs ("If that happened, what would that mean about me?").

DIALECTICAL BEHAVIOR THERAPY (DBT) INTERVENTIONS
1. Distress Tolerance - TIPP Skills (For acute emotional hyperarousal):
- T: Temperature (cold water facial immersion activating the mammalian dive reflex and parasympathetic brake).
- I: Intense Exercise (brief bursts of cardiovascular exertion to metabolize cortisol and adrenaline).
- P: Paced Breathing (prolonged exhalations, 4 seconds in, 7-8 seconds out).
- P: Paired Muscle Relaxation (tensing muscle groups on inhalation, consciously relaxing on exhalation).

2. STOP Skill (Preventing impulsive behavior):
- S: Stop! Do not react or speak.
- T: Take a step back. Take a deep breath.
- O: Observe the internal bodily feelings and external facts.
- P: Proceed mindfully with effective wisdom.

ACCEPTANCE AND COMMITMENT THERAPY (ACT) INTERVENTIONS
1. Cognitive Defusion:
- Stepping back from thoughts rather than fusing with them.
- "I am having the thought that I am unworthy" vs "I am unworthy".
- Physicalizing the thought: naming the thought's shape, weight, color, and treating it as passenger in the car.

2. Values Clarification & Committed Action:
- Disentangling rigid external social expectations from authentic personal values.
- Values are directions (like pointing West), not destinations (goals).
